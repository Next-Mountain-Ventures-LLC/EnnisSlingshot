**An autocycle is a legally distinct three-wheeled vehicle category** — not a car, not a motorcycle — defined by having a steering wheel, seat belts, non-straddle seating, and federal certification to motorcycle safety standards. A Polaris Slingshot is one. Here's the actual statute, why the category exists, and what it means for the license in your wallet.

## What Is an Autocycle? The Legal Definition

Under Texas law, an autocycle is a motor vehicle that meets four specific criteria, per Tex. Transp. Code §501.008(a): three wheels in contact with the ground, seat belts for the operator and any passenger, a steering wheel (not handlebars) for directional control, non-straddle seating, and certification to comply with federal motorcycle safety standards. Meet all four, and the vehicle is legally an autocycle — regardless of how car-like it feels to drive.

## Autocycle vs. Motorcycle: What's the Difference?

The practical differences come down to the controls and the seating. A motorcycle has handlebars and a straddle seat; an autocycle has a steering wheel, foot pedals, and side-by-side seating with seat belts. Here's how the three categories compare on the things that actually matter to a driver:

|  | Autocycle | Motorcycle | Car |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| License (Texas) | Standard Class C | Class M (motorcycle endorsement) | Standard Class C |
| Helmet (Texas) | Required under 21; 21+ exempt with course/insurance | Required under 21; 21+ exempt with course/insurance | Not applicable |
| Seat belts | Yes | No | Yes |
| Registration class | Motorcycle | Motorcycle | Passenger car |

## Is a Polaris Slingshot an Autocycle?

Yes, unambiguously. The Slingshot has three wheels, seat belts, a steering wheel and pedals, side-by-side non-straddle seating, and it's federally certified to motorcycle safety standards — every box on the §501.008(a) test. That's why it's titled and registered as a motorcycle in Texas despite driving nothing like one. Curious whether that makes it street legal everywhere? ## Why Does This Classification Exist? A Short History

Before 2015, a three-wheeled vehicle like the Slingshot didn't fit neatly into existing law in most states — some required a full motorcycle license (Class M) to drive one, despite the car-like controls. Texas created the autocycle category through SB 449 in the 84th Legislature, effective May 22, 2015, specifically to address this mismatch. Once Texas and other states recognized the category, the licensing requirement dropped to a standard Class C license — the change that made vehicles like the Slingshot broadly accessible without requiring riders to get motorcycle-certified first. Most other states followed with similar autocycle laws over the following decade.

## What License Do You Need to Drive an Autocycle in Texas?

A standard Class C driver's license is sufficient under Tex. Transp. Code §521.085(b) — the same license you use to drive any car. No motorcycle endorsement, no separate test. For the complete legal picture — helmet rules, age, insurance, passengers — see our [Texas Slingshot laws guide](https://ennisslingshot.com/blog/texas-slingshot-laws/).

## How Many States Recognize the Autocycle Category?

As of January 2024, 49 states recognize the autocycle category, with Massachusetts as the lone holdout still requiring a motorcycle license. State laws can and do change, so treat this as a snapshot rather than a permanent guarantee — always check your own state's current rules if you're outside Texas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between an autocycle and a motorcycle?

An autocycle has a steering wheel, pedals, seat belts, and side-by-side seating; a motorcycle has handlebars, no seat belts, and a straddle seat. Both are registered as motorcycles in Texas, but the license and helmet rules can differ in practice depending on the vehicle.

### Is a Polaris Slingshot an autocycle?

Yes — it meets all four criteria in Tex. Transp. Code §501.008(a) and is titled and registered as a motorcycle.
