Five things: the Official Bluebonnet City of Texas designation, a major NHRA drag strip, deep Czech heritage and polka roots, a genuinely historic downtown, and — more recently — a guided Slingshot experience through the trails.

## 1. The Official Bluebonnet City of Texas

The 1997 Texas Legislature designated Ennis the home of the Official Texas Bluebonnet Trail and Official Bluebonnet City of Texas. The Ennis Garden Club has mapped and sponsored the trails since 1951 — 40+ miles of routes, open every April 1–30.

## 2. The Ennis Bluebonnet Trails Festival

Held annually, typically the 2nd or 3rd weekend of April (2026 dates were April 17–19; 2027 dates aren't announced yet). Attendance estimates vary by source — Focus Daily News cites 15,000 to 20,000 visitors in a typical year, quoting the city.

## 3. Texas Motorplex — a Major NHRA Drag Racing Venue

Ennis is a motorsports town too. Texas Motorplex was the first all-concrete NHRA "super track," drawing 500,000+ fans a year across a February–November season, including the NHRA Fall Nationals every October.

## 4. Czech Heritage and the National Polka Festival

Ennis's Czech immigrant roots run deep, documented at the Czech Museum and the Ennis Railroad and Cultural Heritage Museum. The National Polka Festival runs every Memorial Day weekend — 13+ bands, up to roughly 50,000 attendees, in its 59th-plus year.

## 5. Historic Downtown Ennis

Brick streets, Dallas Street shopping and dining, the Ennis Public Theatre, Blues on Main (2nd Saturday in June), and a farmers market running April through October. D Magazine ran a full feature, "How to Spend a Day in Ennis," in May 2023 — real outside press coverage of the town beyond the trails.

## A Newer Reason People Are Visiting: a Guided Slingshot Experience

One of five, not the headline: we run the 1-Hour Drive & Go and 2-Hour Bluebonnet Trail Experience through the trails every April, $69.99–$149, no motorcycle license needed, meeting at the Ennis Welcome Center.

## How Big Is Ennis, Texas?

Ennis sits in Ellis County, about 35 miles — roughly 37 minutes — south of downtown Dallas on I-45. Population figures vary by source; check the latest U.S. Census estimate for a current number.
