People ask me how fast a Slingshot goes more than almost anything else. Honest answer: Polaris doesn't publish one clean top-speed number that applies across the lineup. Here's what's actually documented, and what I won't guess at.

## Does Polaris Publish an Official Top Speed?

Not a single consistent figure, no. Polaris publishes horsepower, torque, and a 0–60 time for the R trim — it doesn't put a headline max-mph number on every trim's spec page the way it does for acceleration. I'm not going to repeat a forum number I can't source, so if you see a specific top speed quoted elsewhere without a citation, treat it skeptically.

## 0–60 — The One Sourced Acceleration Figure

The R trim has been independently tested at roughly 4.9 seconds 0–60, per Motorcycle.com's 2021 first-ride review. That's the only trim with an independent number I can point to — I'm not extrapolating it to the S or SL.

## Top Speed vs. 0–60 — Not the Same Thing

These get conflated constantly. 0–60 measures how fast you get up to highway speed; top speed measures the ceiling. A vehicle can be genuinely quick off the line without a high published top end, or the reverse. Don't assume a fast 0–60 implies a specific top speed.

## Can You Drive a Polaris Slingshot on the Highway?

Yes — it's autocycle-classified and road-legal on any public road, no special restriction. That's a legality question, separate from how fast it'll actually go. See our [full Texas Slingshot legal guide](https://ennisslingshot.com/blog/texas-slingshot-laws/) for the complete picture.

## What Affects How Fast It Actually Feels

An open cockpit at 60 mph feels considerably faster than an enclosed car at the same speed — low seating, real wind, no windshield above the dash. Reviewers describe the steering as "razor-sharp" and note it transmits every road imperfection. That sensory intensity is a big part of why people rent one in the first place.
