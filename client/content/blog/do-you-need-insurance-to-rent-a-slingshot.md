## Why a Slingshot Is Legally a Motorcycle for Insurance Purposes

A Slingshot is titled and registered as an autocycle — a motorcycle subclass — under Tex. Transp. Code §501.008, even though it drives like a car and only needs a standard Class C license (§521.085(b)). Those two facts aren't contradictory, but they get confused constantly.

## Does Your Personal Auto Policy Cover It?

Often not — many personal auto policies carry motorcycle or motorsport exclusions that a legally-classified motorcycle would trigger. Policies vary by carrier and state, so check your own policy's exclusions directly rather than assuming either way.

## Does Your Credit Card's Rental Protection Cover It?

Most major card rental-car benefits exclude motorcycles and off-road vehicles per their public terms — check your specific card's benefits guide before assuming coverage extends here.

## What "Insurance Included" Should Actually Mean

It's a vague phrase until you know the deductible amount, what's excluded, and whether it's the vehicle's own insurance or just a rider waiver. Get specifics before you book.

## Questions to Ask Before You Hand Over a Card

What's my maximum out-of-pocket if something happens? Is there a deposit on top of the insurance? What specifically is excluded from coverage?
