Six questions to ask before you book a bluebonnet mini session in Ennis — the logistics most outfit-and-camera guides skip entirely.

## Why Bluebonnet Mini-Session Calendars Fill by January

The bloom typically peaks around the third week of April — a narrow window, which means good photographers' April calendars fill up months ahead.

## Question 1: Is This Public Land or Private Property?

Most roadside bluebonnet fields sit on private land. Ask directly whether your photographer has permission for the specific field they're proposing, and who's liable if it turns out they don't.

## Question 2: What's the Backup-Date Policy If the Bloom Is Late or It Rains?

Bloom timing varies by weather year to year. A photographer without a clear backup-date policy is a real risk for an outdoor-dependent session.

## Question 3: What's Included?

Ask for the edited image count, turnaround time, and print rights up front — all three vary widely between photographers.

## Question 4: Where Do They Shoot in Ennis, and Have They Shot Here Before?

Local experience matters for a field-based shoot — a photographer who knows the Ennis trail system already knows where the light and access actually work.

## Question 5: What's the Cancellation and Rescheduling Policy?

Get this in writing before you pay anything.

## Question 6: How Do Payment and Deposits Work?

Mini session pricing in Texas commonly runs roughly $150–$400 depending on the photographer and package — confirm directly with yours.

## Pairing Your Mini Session With an Ennis Day Trip

Trail maps are available at the Ennis Welcome Center, 201 NW Main St. Many families pair their photo session with a trip downtown or a Slingshot golden-hour ride — we don't offer photography ourselves, but the two pair naturally into one Ennis day.
