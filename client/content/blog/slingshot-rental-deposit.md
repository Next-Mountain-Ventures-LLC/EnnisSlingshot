Deposits across the category range from $75 to $1,500 depending on the operator. Ours is $0. Here's what the terms actually mean and how to read any operator's fine print.

## Deposit vs. Deductible vs. Damage Waiver — What Each Term Means

A deposit is money held (or authorized on a card) upfront, refunded if nothing happens. A deductible is your maximum out-of-pocket if something does happen. A damage waiver is an optional add-on that reduces or eliminates that deductible. Operators use these terms inconsistently, so always ask which one you're actually being quoted.

## Why Deposits Exist

Rental businesses price for risk. A deposit or high deductible shifts that risk onto the renter; comprehensive insurance shifts more of it onto the operator's policy instead. Neither approach is wrong — they're just different ways of allocating the same risk.

## What Happens If You Actually Damage a Rental Slingshot

Typically: report it immediately, the operator assesses the damage, and whatever deductible or deposit terms apply get triggered. We don't have a source for an "average" damage cost industry-wide — ask any operator for their damage-cost history in writing rather than guessing.

## Age-Based Deposit Tiers

Several operators charge more for renters under 25 (or under 28) — standard risk-pricing, same logic insurers use for young drivers generally.

## Questions to Ask Any Operator Before You Book

Is this a deposit, a deductible, or both? What's my actual maximum exposure if something happens? Is insurance included or a separate add-on? How long does deposit refund take?
