People ask me all the time whether "Bluebonnet Capital of Texas" is just something Ennis calls itself. It isn't. The state legislature made it official in 1997.

## The 1997 Designation

The Texas Legislature named Ennis home of the Official Texas Bluebonnet Trail and the Official Bluebonnet City of Texas in 1997, per Ellis County's own government records. That's a specific legislative act, not a chamber-of-commerce slogan.

## What Ennis Had Already Built by 1997

The state didn't hand Ennis this designation out of nowhere. By 1997, the Ennis Garden Club had been mapping and maintaining bluebonnet driving trails for 45 years — I go into that full origin story in a [separate post](https://ennisslingshot.com/blog/the-founding-of-the-ennis-bluebonnet-trails-how-a-1951-idea-became-40-miles-of-roads/). By the time the legislature acted, Ennis was already an established regional draw.

## Is Ennis the Only "Bluebonnet Capital"?

Honest answer: no, not in casual usage. Burnet, TX also gets called a bluebonnet capital in some regional coverage. Here's the distinction that actually matters: Ennis's designation came from a specific act of the Texas Legislature. Other towns' "bluebonnet capital" branding tends to be informal or chamber-level. For the full Ennis-vs-Burnet comparison, see our [Bluebonnet Capital of Texas guide](https://ennisslingshot.com/bluebonnets/bluebonnet-capital-of-texas/).

## What the Designation Means Today

Practically, it shows up in the town's signage, its marketing, and the continued growth of the Ennis Bluebonnet Festival. The Welcome Center — where every one of my rides stages — is the town's front door for all of it.

## The Trails and Festival Today

The trails themselves run April 1–30 every year. The festival lands mid-April — the 2027 festival is expected April 17–19, 2027 (dates subject to change). For full current logistics, see our [complete Ennis Bluebonnet Trails guide](https://ennisslingshot.com/bluebonnets/).
