Ennis is the closer trail if you're DFW-based; Chappell Hill is closer if you're near Houston or Austin. Both are worth the drive — here's an honest, non-disparaging comparison to help you pick.

## Ennis — The DFW-Area Trail

Ennis sits 35 miles — about 37 minutes — south of downtown Dallas on I-45. The Ennis Garden Club maps 40+ miles across three official trail routes, and the Texas Legislature designated Ennis the state's Official Bluebonnet City in 1997. The trails run April 1–30 only.

## Chappell Hill — The Houston/Austin-Area Trail

Chappell Hill sits at US-290 and FM 1155, about 57 miles northwest of Houston — roughly 1 to 1.5 hours depending on traffic. Its Bluebonnet Festival, run by the Chappell Hill Historical Society, bills itself as the "Official Bluebonnet Festival of Texas" and takes place the second weekend of April, drawing 250+ juried artisans. The Brenham/Washington County Chamber's Wildflower Watch tracks real-time blooms across 70+ miles of driving routes through Independence and Burton, with peak bloom typically early-to-mid April. Source: Chappell Hill Historical Society, chappellhillhistoricalsociety.com.

## Trail Experience — What's Actually Different

Ennis runs three official Garden Club–mapped loops, a defined system built specifically for self-guided driving. Chappell Hill centers on a festival weekend plus a broader 70+ mile driving network through Washington County towns like Independence and Burton — less a single mapped loop, more a wildflower-watch region.

## So Which Should You Drive?

If you're DFW-based, Ennis is the obvious pick on drive time alone. If you're Houston- or Austin-based, Chappell Hill is closer and has the bigger festival draw. If you want to see both in the same spring, Ennis's trails open the full month of April while Chappell Hill's festival is a single weekend — plan Chappell Hill's festival weekend first, then make the Ennis trip any other April weekend.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is better, Ennis or Chappell Hill bluebonnets?

Neither is objectively better — it depends where you're driving from. DFW-based, Ennis wins on distance. Houston/Austin-based, Chappell Hill wins on distance.
