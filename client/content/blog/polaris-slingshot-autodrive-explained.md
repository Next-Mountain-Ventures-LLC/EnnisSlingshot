Let's correct something by name: AutoDrive is not a CVT. I've seen it described that way plenty of times. Here's what it actually is.

## Is the Polaris Slingshot Automatic?

Yes, when equipped with AutoDrive — gas and brake pedals only, no clutch pedal, no manual shift lever.

## What AutoDrive Actually Is (Not a CVT)

AutoDrive is a hydraulically actuated automated 5-speed — mechanically, it's an automated manual gearbox, closer to a dual-clutch setup than a belt-and-pulley CVT. The computer selects and executes gear changes that a human would otherwise handle with a clutch and shifter. A true CVT has no discrete gears at all; AutoDrive has five real ones, shifted for you.

## Does the Slingshot Have a Clutch?

Not for the driver — no clutch pedal on AutoDrive-equipped units.

## When AutoDrive Was Introduced

Polaris introduced AutoDrive for the 2020 model year alongside the ProStar 2.0L engine redesign, replacing the prior generation's GM 2.4L Ecotec/manual-only setup. If you're looking at a pre-2020 Slingshot, this doesn't apply the same way.

## What It Feels Like to Drive — the Downshift/Engine-Braking Tip

AutoDrive downshifts under braking similarly to how a manual would — it feels like engine braking. First-timers who "fight" the transmission are almost always just unfamiliar with that sensation, not experiencing a malfunction. Let it do its job.

## AutoDrive vs. Manual — Which Should You Choose?

Depends who's asking. For a rental or first-timer context, AutoDrive is the only sensible choice — it's what makes a no-experience rental possible in the first place. For a buyer cross-shopping trims, manual is available on several trims for anyone who specifically wants direct control; I won't claim deep expertise on manual-trim driving dynamics beyond that, since it's not what we run.
