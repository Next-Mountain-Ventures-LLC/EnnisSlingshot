## The best things to do in Ennis, TX, ranked

Ennis, Texas sits in Ellis County about 35 miles south of downtown Dallas on I-45 — close enough for a day trip, distinctive enough to fill a whole weekend. The town is best known as the Official Bluebonnet City of Texas, home to the Ennis Bluebonnet Trails and Festival every April, but it also hosts an NHRA-sanctioned drag strip, a Czech-heritage festival with roots going back decades, a walkable historic downtown, and a lake with room for the whole family. Below are more than 20 things to do in and around Ennis, ranked roughly by how often visitors seek them out, with the best time of year to go.

We built this list around what actually shows up in visitor searches, city and tourism sources, and outside coverage of Ennis — not a generic "top things to do" template. That's why the bluebonnet-related entries sit at the top: they're genuinely what most people are looking for when they search for Ennis in the first place. Everything below the fold still holds up the rest of the year, whether or not you're chasing wildflowers.

### 1. Ennis Bluebonnet Trails

The reason most people find Ennis in the first place. The Ennis Garden Club maintains more than 40 miles of mapped driving loops through the surrounding countryside, open every April, with peak bloom typically landing around the third week of the month. It's the oldest bluebonnet trail known in Texas, and the 1997 Texas Legislature named Ennis the state's Official Bluebonnet City for it. Maps are handed out at the Ennis Welcome Center, 201 NW Main St, which is also where the trails effectively begin, and the city's free Ennis Y'all app adds a GPS-based version of the same map for anyone navigating from a phone. Most visitors drive the loops in their own car, but the roads themselves — narrow, winding, mostly rural two-lanes — are also what makes them a genuinely good driving route, not just a scenic backdrop. For the full loop breakdown, bloom timing, and photo spots, see our [Ennis Bluebonnet Trails & Festival guide](/bluebonnets/).

### 2. Ennis Bluebonnet Trails Festival

The trails' companion event, typically held mid-April in downtown Ennis (historically the second or third weekend; 2027 dates hadn't been announced as of this writing). Recent years have run Friday and Saturday 10 am–9 pm and Sunday 10 am–6 pm, with a modest gate admission and free entry for young kids. Expect a parade, live music, arts and crafts vendors, food, and a genuinely packed downtown — this is the busiest weekend of the Ennis year, so plan parking and timing accordingly, and arrive early if you want to avoid the tightest crowds. Details, when announced, will be on the [bluebonnets hub](/bluebonnets/).

### 3. Historic Downtown Ennis & Dallas Street

Ennis's downtown historic district centers on Dallas Street and the surrounding blocks — brick storefronts, local shops, and a walkable grid that's easy to cover in an hour or stretch into an afternoon. It's the town's default "what to do between the big events" answer, home to several of the restaurants on our [where to eat](/ennis/where-to-eat/) page and worth pairing with the museums a few blocks away. Unlike the seasonal attractions on this list, downtown works year-round, which makes it the easiest recommendation on this whole page regardless of when you're visiting. Full guide: [Downtown Ennis](/ennis/downtown/).

### 4. Texas Motorplex

An all-concrete NHRA "super track" and one of the sport's premier drag strips, drawing more than 500,000 fans a year across a season that runs roughly February through November. The NHRA Fall Nationals in October is the marquee weekend, and search interest in the track itself peaks in both March and October — the two months bracketing its busiest racing. Even on a non-race day, it's worth knowing what else is nearby if you're in town for the track. Full guide: [Texas Motorplex](/ennis/texas-motorplex/).

### 5. Drive a Slingshot on the trails

We'll be straightforward about this one since we run it: [Ennis Slingshot Experience](/slingshot-rental/) offers 1–2 hour self-drive experiences on the Bluebonnet Trails in a Polaris Slingshot, an open-cockpit three-wheeler that drives like a car — no motorcycle license needed, insurance included. Packages start at $69.99 for a one-hour Drive & Go and run to $149 for a two-hour, two-person Bluebonnet Trail Experience, with every rental starting at the Ennis Welcome Center. It's a seasonal, April-only experience, and it's one option among many on this list, not the only reason to visit Ennis. If you're already coming for the bluebonnets, it's a way to see the trails from behind the wheel instead of through a windshield. See the [Bluebonnet Trail Experience](/slingshot-rental/bluebonnet-trail-experience/) for details.

### 6. National Polka Festival

A Memorial Day weekend tradition rooted in Ennis's Czech and Moravian heritage, with 13 or more polka bands, a parade, and Czech food throughout downtown. City and festival sources put attendance in the tens of thousands, with the festival now in its sixth decade. It's a completely different flavor of Ennis than April's bluebonnets — later spring, different crowd, and a genuinely distinct piece of Texas cultural history that's easy to miss if you only associate Ennis with wildflowers. Full guide: [National Polka Festival](/ennis/national-polka-festival/).

### 7. Ennis Railroad & Cultural Heritage Museum

Housed in a 1915 former restaurant building in downtown Ennis (105 NE Main St), this museum covers the town's origins as a Houston & Texas Central Railroad stop and its Czech immigrant history, with railroad memorabilia, depot replicas, and vintage Czech artifacts. Ennis itself is named for a director of the Houston & Texas Central Railroad, and the museum leans into that history alongside the waves of Czech immigrants who arrived once the rail line was in place. It's a compact, well-curated stop that pairs naturally with a downtown walk.

### 8. Czech Museum (Sokol Hall)

A smaller, appointment-based counterpart to the Railroad & Cultural Heritage Museum, housed inside the Sokol Hall Activity Center. It holds Czech costumes, books, dolls, and other pieces tied to the wave of Czech and Moravian immigrants who settled Ellis County in the 1870s and after — the same heritage the National Polka Festival celebrates every May. Because it's appointment-based rather than open daily, it's best planned ahead rather than added as a spontaneous stop.

### 9. Lake Bardwell

A roughly 3,500-acre US Army Corps of Engineers lake just outside Ennis with more than 150,000 visitors a year, drawing fishing, boating, hiking, and picnicking crowds. Several of the roads that skirt the lake — including the Lakeview Drive area — are also part of the Bluebonnet Trail system, so an April visit can combine both, and the lake stays a solid outdoor option in every other month when the wildflowers aren't the draw.

### 10. Meadow View Nature Area

A 39-acre nature area with a Lake Bardwell backdrop that becomes one of the most photographed bluebonnet spots in Ennis every April. It's open seasonally rather than being a year-round attraction, so time a visit for the trail window — go in the first hour after sunrise or the last before sunset if photos are the goal, when the light is softer and the crowds are thinner.

### 11. Bluebonnet Park

A 47-acre city park that doubles as a reliable bluebonnet photo location in April and a regular city park the rest of the year — shade, open space, and easy access if you're staying downtown. It's a good backup if Meadow View feels crowded on a festival weekend, since it's closer to downtown and easier to reach on foot.

### 12. Sugar Ridge Winery

A family-run winery in Bristol, just off I-45 on Sugar Ridge Road (part of the north Bluebonnet Trail loop), known for handcrafted, fruit-forward wines and a patio with live music on weekends. It only opens Saturday and Sunday, so plan around that if it's on your list. It's the most frequently mentioned Ennis-area business in outside bluebonnet coverage — worth a stop after, not before, any driving.

### 13. Galaxy Drive-In

A classic drive-in movie theater that keeps the format alive for Ennis and the surrounding towns — a straightforward, nostalgic evening option if you're staying overnight and want something low-key after a full day of driving trails or walking downtown.

### 14. Big O Speedway

A dirt track running a March-through-October season, drawing local short-track racing fans. A different, grittier motorsports scene than Texas Motorplex's drag strip — oval racing instead of a quarter-mile — and worth checking if you're already a racing crowd visiting for the Motorplex and want a second event on the same trip.

### 15. Ennis Public Theatre

The town's community theater, staging seasonal productions throughout the year — check current listings if you're planning an evening in town beyond dinner. It's a reminder that Ennis has a genuine year-round cultural calendar, not just its two headline festivals.

### 16. Blues on Main

An annual downtown music event held the second Saturday in June, bringing live blues to Ennis's Main Street district — a good excuse for a summer visit outside bluebonnet season, when the trails themselves are long since past their April bloom.

### 17. Downtown Farmers Market

Ennis runs a downtown farmers market from April through October, a low-key way to sample the town on a Saturday morning without a festival crowd — local produce, a handful of vendors, and none of the parking hassle of festival weekend.

### 18. Kachina Prairie

A native-prairie site near Ennis that hosts guided wildflower walks in April, appealing to visitors who want a slower, more naturalist-led look at the bluebonnet season than the driving trails offer — a good pairing for anyone who wants to walk the prairie one day and drive the trails the next.

### 19. Waxahachie & Scarborough Renaissance Festival

Waxahachie is about 15 minutes northwest of Ennis and home to the Scarborough Renaissance Festival, a 35-acre Renaissance village with roughly 200 shoppes that runs weekends from April through May plus Memorial Day. Waxahachie's own historic courthouse square and downtown are worth a stop in their own right, festival or not. Its season overlaps almost exactly with the Bluebonnet Trails, making an Ennis-plus-Waxahachie weekend one of the most natural pairings in the region. Full guide: [Ennis + Waxahachie weekend itinerary](/ennis/weekend-itinerary/).

### 20. Corsicana

About 18 miles south of Ennis on I-45, Corsicana adds the Collin Street Bakery (famous nationally for its fruitcake), Pioneer Village, and the Pearce Museum area to a longer day trip south — a good option if you want to extend an Ennis visit rather than double back to Dallas, especially if you're already road-tripping down I-45 for a multi-stop weekend.

## Planning your visit

Most of what's on this list clusters around two seasons: April (bluebonnets, the festival, Scarborough Faire's opening weekends) and race weekends at Texas Motorplex (peak in March and October) or Memorial Day (the National Polka Festival). Outside of those windows, downtown Ennis, its museums, and Lake Bardwell are dependable year-round options. ## FAQ

**What is Ennis, TX known for?**
Ennis is the Texas Legislature's designated Official Bluebonnet City of Texas and home to the Ennis Bluebonnet Trails — more than 40 miles of mapped driving loops that peak in April. It's also home to Texas Motorplex, an NHRA drag-racing venue, and the National Polka Festival, a Czech-heritage celebration held every Memorial Day weekend.

**What is the best time of year to visit Ennis, TX?**
April is Ennis's signature month — the Bluebonnet Trails are open April 1–30, bloom typically peaks around the third week, and the Ennis Bluebonnet Trails Festival caps the season. October (Texas Motorplex's Fall Nationals) and Memorial Day weekend (National Polka Festival) are the next-best windows if bluebonnets aren't the draw.

**Is Ennis, TX worth a day trip from Dallas?**
Yes. Ennis is about 35 miles south of downtown Dallas on I-45, roughly a 37-minute drive, which makes it an easy half-day or full-day trip. See our [hour-by-hour Dallas-to-Ennis itinerary](/ennis/day-trip-from-dallas/) for how to structure the visit.

**What's a fun, slightly different thing to do in Ennis?**
Drive yourself along the Bluebonnet Trails in a Polaris Slingshot. [Ennis Slingshot Experience](/slingshot-rental/) runs 1–2 hour self-drive experiences from the Ennis Welcome Center during April, with insurance included and no motorcycle license required.
