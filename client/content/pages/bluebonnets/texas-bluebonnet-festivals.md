## Texas bluebonnet festivals, compared

If you're deciding which Texas bluebonnet festival to visit, distance from home and what each event actually offers matter more than which one claims the biggest crowd — and for most DFW-area readers, that distance question has a pretty clear answer. Here's an honest side-by-side, built from each town's own materials and regional coverage — we mark dates "typically" unless we have a specific sourced year to point to.

| Festival | Typical timing | Distance from Dallas | What it's known for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ennis Bluebonnet Trails Festival** | Mid-April, historically 2nd–3rd weekend | ~35 mi / ~37 min | 40+ miles of mapped driving trails, 1997 state designation, downtown vendor market and car show |
| **Burnet Bluebonnet Festival** | Typically April | Considerably farther than Ennis (Central Texas, outside easy DFW day-trip range) | Its own Legislature-granted bluebonnet title; Hill Country setting |
| **Chappell Hill Bluebonnet Festival** | Typically April | Farther south, closer to the Houston/Austin corridor than DFW | A well-known Central Texas bluebonnet event with its own long history |

For DFW-based readers, this comparison mostly comes down to one variable: Ennis is dramatically closer, and it's the only one on this list with a formal 40-plus-mile mapped trail system to drive, not just a festival weekend. See our [full pillar guide](/bluebonnets/) for everything the Ennis trails and festival include.

## Ennis Bluebonnet Trails Festival

Historically the second or third weekend of April; in 2026 it ran April 17–19 with $5 adult admission, kids 12 and under free, and a cashless gate. The festival is a separate ticketed event from the free, month-long trails themselves. Full schedule, ticket, and parking detail is on our [dedicated festival page](/bluebonnets/festival/).

## Burnet Bluebonnet Festival

Burnet, TX in the Texas Hill Country holds its own Legislature-granted bluebonnet designation from an earlier resolution, distinct from Ennis's 1997 title — which is part of why the two towns are sometimes referred to together as "co-capitals" in coverage like Texas Highways. Burnet's festival typically runs in April; check Burnet's own event listings for confirmed current-year dates rather than relying on a prior-year pattern, since we don't have the same multi-year data for Burnet that we do for Ennis. From the Dallas–Fort Worth metro, Burnet is a considerably longer drive than Ennis, putting it outside easy day-trip range for most DFW visitors.

## Chappell Hill Bluebonnet Festival

Chappell Hill, in the corridor between Houston and Austin, runs its own well-established bluebonnet festival, typically in April. It's a reasonable option for Texans based closer to Houston or Austin, but it's a longer trip than either Ennis or Burnet for anyone starting from Dallas–Fort Worth, and not one we'd recommend as a primary destination for a DFW-based day trip given the distance involved.

## Other Texas bluebonnet events worth knowing about

Beyond the three festivals compared above, bluebonnet season generates activity across much of Texas in a good year — roadside wildflower drives, smaller-town garden club events, and informal photo-tourism around fields that never developed a formal festival at all. We haven't attempted to catalog every one of these, since most don't have the mapped trail infrastructure, sourced visitor data, or multi-decade track record that Ennis, Burnet, and Chappell Hill do. If a bluebonnet event near you isn't listed here, that's a gap in our research rather than a judgment that it isn't worth visiting — Texas wildflower season is genuinely widespread.

## Planning a multi-festival bluebonnet trip

Because bluebonnet season runs statewide through most of April, it's technically possible to build a longer trip hitting more than one of these festivals, though the driving distances involved (Ennis to Burnet is a multi-hour trip on its own) make it a bigger undertaking than a single day trip. For most DFW-based visitors, we'd suggest treating Ennis as the anchor of a single-day or overnight trip — see our [weekend itinerary](/ennis/weekend-itinerary/) for pairing it with nearby Waxahachie — rather than trying to string together festivals hours apart in one weekend.

## Why we compare rather than just claim "biggest"

You'll see attendance claims attached to several of these events, but they come from a mix of city estimates, promoter listings, and unsourced snippets that don't always agree with each other — the same pattern we found even within Ennis's own reporting (see our [pillar guide's](/bluebonnets/) discussion of the 15,000–35,000 range across different sources). Rather than repeat an unverifiable "biggest in Texas" claim for any of these festivals, we've focused this comparison on the things that are actually verifiable: typical timing, distance, and what infrastructure each town maintains — trails versus a single festival weekend.

For most DFW-based readers, distance settles the question: Ennis is roughly 35 miles and 37 minutes from downtown Dallas, while Burnet and Chappell Hill are both considerably farther and outside comfortable day-trip range for a single afternoon.
