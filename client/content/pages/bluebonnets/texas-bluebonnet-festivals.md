## Texas bluebonnet festivals, compared

If you're deciding which Texas bluebonnet festival to visit, distance from home and what each event actually offers matter more than which one claims the biggest crowd — and for most DFW-area readers, that distance question has a pretty clear answer. Here's a side-by-side look at the best-known Texas bluebonnet festivals.

| Festival | Timing | Distance from Dallas | What it's known for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ennis Bluebonnet Trails Festival** | April 17–19, 2027 (expected; subject to change) | ~35 mi / ~37 min | 40+ miles of mapped driving trails, 1997 state designation, downtown vendor market and car show |
| **Burnet Bluebonnet Festival** | Typically April | Considerably farther than Ennis (Central Texas, outside easy DFW day-trip range) | Its own Legislature-granted bluebonnet title; Hill Country setting |
| **Chappell Hill Bluebonnet Festival** | Typically April | Farther south, closer to the Houston/Austin corridor than DFW | A well-known Central Texas bluebonnet event with its own long history |

For DFW-based readers, this comparison mostly comes down to one variable: Ennis is dramatically closer, and it's the only one on this list with a formal 40-plus-mile mapped trail system to drive, not just a festival weekend. See our [complete Ennis Bluebonnet Trails guide](/bluebonnets/) for everything the Ennis trails and festival include.

## Ennis Bluebonnet Trails Festival

The 2027 festival is expected April 17–19, 2027 (dates subject to change), with the trails open April 1–30, 2027. In 2026 it ran April 17–19 with $5 adult admission, kids 12 and under free, and a cashless gate. The festival is a separate ticketed event from the free, month-long trails themselves. Full schedule, ticket, and parking detail is on our [dedicated festival page](/bluebonnets/festival/).

## Burnet Bluebonnet Festival

Burnet, TX in the Texas Hill Country holds its own Legislature-granted bluebonnet designation from an earlier resolution, distinct from Ennis's 1997 title — which is part of why the two towns are sometimes referred to together as "co-capitals" in coverage like Texas Highways. Burnet's festival typically runs in April; check Burnet's own event listings for confirmed current-year dates. From the Dallas–Fort Worth metro, Burnet is a considerably longer drive than Ennis, putting it outside easy day-trip range for most DFW visitors.

## Chappell Hill Bluebonnet Festival

Chappell Hill, in the corridor between Houston and Austin, runs its own well-established bluebonnet festival, typically in April. It's a reasonable option for Texans based closer to Houston or Austin, but it's a longer trip than either Ennis or Burnet for anyone starting from Dallas–Fort Worth, and not one we'd recommend as a primary destination for a DFW-based day trip given the distance involved.

## Other Texas bluebonnet events worth knowing about

Beyond the three festivals compared above, bluebonnet season generates activity across much of Texas in a good year — roadside wildflower drives, smaller-town garden club events, and informal photo-tourism around fields that never developed a formal festival at all. Most of these don't have the mapped trails or multi-decade track record of Ennis, Burnet, and Chappell Hill, but they're worth a stop if one is near you — Texas wildflower season is genuinely widespread.

## Planning a multi-festival bluebonnet trip

Because bluebonnet season runs statewide through most of April, it's technically possible to build a longer trip hitting more than one of these festivals, though the driving distances involved (Ennis to Burnet is a multi-hour trip on its own) make it a bigger undertaking than a single day trip. For most DFW-based visitors, we'd suggest treating Ennis as the anchor of a single-day or overnight trip — see our [weekend itinerary](/ennis/weekend-itinerary/) for pairing it with nearby Waxahachie — rather than trying to string together festivals hours apart in one weekend.

## Which bluebonnet festival is biggest?

Attendance claims for Texas bluebonnet festivals vary widely, and none of these events publishes an official count. Ennis draws tens of thousands of visitors every April (see our [complete Ennis Bluebonnet Trails guide](/bluebonnets/) for the numbers), but the more useful comparison is what each town offers: typical timing, distance from home, and whether there's a full trail system to drive or just a festival weekend.
