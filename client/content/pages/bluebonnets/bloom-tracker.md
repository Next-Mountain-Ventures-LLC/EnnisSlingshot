## Is it peak bloom right now?

Check the tracker above for this week's status by loop. This page explains how bloom timing works in Ennis, what "peak" actually means, and how we source and report our weekly updates — it's the background page, not the live status itself, which updates during the season.

## How bloom timing works

Bluebonnet season in Ennis generally runs from **late March into early May**, with color typically **peaking around the third week of April**, per reporting from the [Ennis Garden Club](https://bluebonnettrail.org/trailmap). But that's a pattern, not a guarantee — exact timing moves around from year to year based on rainfall the previous fall and winter. Bluebonnet seeds germinate in the fall, so a wet fall tends to set up a fuller, more predictable bloom the following spring, while a dry fall and a warm winter tend to produce a lighter, earlier-fading season. Statewide forecasters described the 2026 season as "moderate" heading in for exactly that reason, following a dry fall and warm winter that produced some early blooms.

Because the trigger conditions happen months before the flowers do, nobody — including us — can reliably forecast bloom quality from the prior summer. The honest answer to "will this be a good year" is: check closer to the season, and check this page during the season itself rather than trying to plan around a guess made in September.

## What "peak" actually means

"Peak bloom" doesn't mean one specific calendar day. It means the largest share of bluebonnet plants along a given stretch of road are flowering simultaneously, producing the densest color for photos. In a typical year, peak conditions hold for roughly a week to ten days on a given loop before flowers begin fading, and different loops can peak at slightly different times depending on terrain, sun exposure, and local rainfall variation. That's why we report status **by loop**, not as one number for the entire trail system — the North Loop might be at peak while the South Loop is still a week out.

We use a simple status scale for each loop:

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| Not started | Little to no visible bloom yet |
| Early | Scattered blooms appearing, not yet dense |
| Peak | Dense, widespread color — the best window for photos |
| Fading | Blooms thinning out, still visible but past their best |
| Past | Season effectively over for that loop |

## Why bloom timing varies loop by loop

The three official loops — North, South, and West — don't all peak on the same day, even in the same year. Terrain, sun exposure, elevation, and even how close a stretch of road sits to a creek bottom versus open ranchland all affect when a given field comes into full color. That's the main reason we report status separately for each loop instead of a single trail-system-wide reading: a visitor asking "should I go this weekend" gets a much more useful answer from "the West Loop is at peak, the South Loop is still early" than from a single average across all three.

It also means a strategy of driving whichever loop is currently strongest, rather than picking one in advance and sticking to it regardless of conditions, tends to produce better results for photo-focused visits. Check the [trail map](/bluebonnets/trail-map/) alongside this tracker so a "peak on the West Loop" reading translates into an actual route you can follow.

## What affects bloom quality beyond timing

Timing (when peak happens) and quality (how dense and vivid the bloom actually is) are two different things, and both matter. A season can arrive right on schedule in mid-April but still be a lighter, more scattered bloom if fall and winter rainfall was low — this is what forecasters meant describing 2026 as a "moderate" season heading in, following a dry fall and warm winter. Conversely, a wet fall and winter can produce an unusually dense, vivid season even if the peak date itself lands close to the historical average. We factor both timing and density into our weekly status notes rather than reporting timing alone.

## How we source and report each update

Our weekly status comes from two places: the [Ennis Garden Club's](https://bluebonnettrail.org/) own weekly reports, which the club posts to the Welcome Center and to bluebonnettrail.org after driving the loops themselves, and our own observations from guests riding with us on the trails. We don't extrapolate from prior years or guess based on the calendar — if we haven't gotten a current-week read from either source, we say so rather than posting a stale status as current.

During the active season (roughly mid-March through early May) we aim to update this page weekly. Outside that window, expect the tracker to show "not started" or "past" rather than an active reading.
