## April climate normals for Ennis

Bluebonnet season falls almost entirely within April, and the climate pattern is consistent enough to plan around months in advance:

| Metric | Typical value |
|---|---|
| Average high | 78°F |
| Average low | 55°F |
| Chance of measurable rain on a given day | ~26% |

These figures are climate normals — long-run averages, not a forecast for any specific date — compiled from Wanderlog/WeatherSpark. In practical terms: expect comfortable daytime temperatures most of the month, cool mornings that warrant a light layer, and roughly a one-in-four chance any given day brings some rain. That's not high enough to plan your whole trip around avoiding rain, but high enough that packing for it makes sense.

## Does rain affect the bluebonnets themselves

A rainy day during the trip doesn't damage the flowers — bluebonnets are a spring wildflower adapted to April showers, and a shower can actually extend the bloom window by keeping plants hydrated. What actually determines bloom quality is rainfall the **previous fall and winter**, since that's when bluebonnet seeds germinate underground before flowering in spring. A dry fall and warm winter tend to produce a lighter, earlier-fading season regardless of how much it rains in April itself. For current bloom conditions by loop, check the [Bloom Tracker](/bluebonnets/bloom-tracker/) rather than trying to infer bloom quality from the weather forecast.

## How April weather compares to March and May

Bluebonnet season isn't strictly limited to April — blooms can start in late March and linger into early May depending on the year — so it's worth knowing how the shoulder months compare. Early spring mornings in late March tend to run cooler than the April average, with a higher chance of a genuinely cold snap, while early May afternoons can push warmer than the April high as Texas heads into summer. If you're planning a trip outside the core April window, expect the general shape of the forecast to hold — comfortable afternoons, cool mornings, and periodic rain chances — but check the live forecast widget rather than relying on April-specific normals for a late-March or early-May date.

## Sun exposure on the trails

Because most of the trail loops run through open ranchland with minimal tree cover, sun exposure is more intense than the temperature alone suggests — even on a 78°F day, standing in an open field for photos can feel considerably warmer than the ambient reading. This is worth factoring in alongside the temperature and rain data above: sunscreen and sunglasses matter on nearly every visit, not just the hottest days.

## What to wear and pack

Given the 78°F/55°F swing between afternoon and early morning, and the roughly one-in-four chance of rain:

- **Layers** — a light jacket or long sleeves for the morning, shed by afternoon
- **Sunscreen and sunglasses** — open roadside fields have little shade
- **Closed-toe shoes** — most photo stops are roadside grass or gravel, not paved
- **A light rain layer** — not required most days, but worth having given the odds
- **Bug spray** — fire ants are common in roadside grass during warm months

## Wind and its effect on an open-cockpit ride

Temperature and rain get most of the attention, but wind matters just as much for anyone riding in an open-cockpit vehicle like a Polaris Slingshot. A breezy but dry day can feel considerably cooler than the thermometer suggests once you're moving at speed, so even on a warm afternoon a light jacket is worth having in the car. Genuinely high wind — not just a breezy day, but sustained strong gusts — is one of the conditions we watch for separately from rain when deciding whether a ride is safe to run.
