## Structure
Answer-first intro (~130w, TL;DR box: the short version of all 12 verdicts in one scannable list) · then 12 short H2/H3 myth blocks (each ~100–130w: the myth stated, the one-sentence verdict, the supporting fact + citation, a link to the deeper post where one exists):
1. Myth: "You need a motorcycle license to drive one." Verdict: False in Texas — a standard Class C license is enough (Tex. Transp. Code §521.085(b)). Link: `/blog/texas-slingshot-laws/`.
2. Myth: "It's basically a three-wheeled ATV." Verdict: False — it's a street-legal autocycle with a steering wheel, pedals, and seat belts, titled/registered as a motorcycle but built for paved roads. Link: `/blog/is-a-polaris-slingshot-street-legal/`.
3. Myth: "It tips over easily in corners." Verdict: False as marketed — standard ABS, traction control, and electronic stability control plus a wide "tadpole" three-wheel stance (two front, one rear) are engineered against rollover; cite attributed safety framing, not an original claim. Link: `/blog/polaris-slingshot-brakes/`.
4. Myth: "It's a 1.5-liter twin-cylinder engine." Verdict: False — every Slingshot since MY2020 uses the Polaris ProStar 2.0L inline-four, 180 hp (S/SL) or up to 204 hp (SLR/R/Signature/Grand Touring). Link: `/blog/polaris-slingshot-101/`.
5. Myth: "It's illegal to drive in Texas." Verdict: False — legal in Texas since 2015 under the autocycle classification (SB 449). Link: `/blog/texas-slingshot-laws/`.
6. Myth: "Only experienced motorcyclists can drive it." Verdict: False — AutoDrive means no clutch or manual shifting; "if you can drive a car, you can drive a Slingshot" is the standard operator line, and Polaris's own data shows most new Slingshot customers were new to Polaris. Link: `/blog/polaris-slingshot-101/`.
7. Myth: "You have to wear a helmet by law." Verdict: Partially true — riders under 21 must; 21+ are exempt with a safety course or qualifying health insurance (Tex. Transp. Code §661.003), though most operators require one anyway. Link: `/blog/do-i-need-a-helmet-for-a-polaris-slingshot/`.
8. Myth: "It's the same as the amusement-park Slingshot ride." Verdict: False — pure keyword confusion; this is a self-driven street vehicle, not a reverse-bungee launch ride. No link needed (disambiguation only).
9. Myth: "It can't handle rain." Verdict: Overstated — it has no roof, so you will get wetter than a car but not as wet as an unprotected motorcyclist; water-resistant interior and gasketed storage are standard. No dedicated post exists yet — cite `audience-market-research.md` §2d directly.
10. Myth: "It has airbags and a roll cage like a real car." Verdict: False — no airbags, no crumple zone; there's a rear roll hoop (not a cage). State plainly, don't soften. Link: `/blog/polaris-slingshot-101/` (Section 7).
11. Myth: "It's a manual transmission you have to learn to shift." Verdict: False for AutoDrive-equipped units — hydraulically actuated automated 5-speed, no clutch pedal. Link: `/blog/polaris-slingshot-101/` (Section 5).
12. Myth: "Renting one means a big deposit and insurance hassle." Verdict: False at Ennis Slingshot specifically — comprehensive insurance included, no deposit, $500 max out-of-pocket. This is our own policy, not a Slingshot-wide fact — say so explicitly. Link: `/slingshot-rental/requirements/`.
