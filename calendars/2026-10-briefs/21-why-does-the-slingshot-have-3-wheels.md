## Facts & sources to cite
- Polaris Slingshot uses a reverse-trike ("tadpole") layout: two wheels in front, one driven wheel in back — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris_Slingshot
- Federally classified as a motorcycle; recognized as an "autocycle" in 49 states as of Jan 2024 (Massachusetts the lone holdout) — Wikipedia (per `seo/research/audience-market-research.md` §6c)
- Tex. Transp. Code §501.008(a) defines an autocycle as a vehicle with "not more than three wheels on the ground," seat belts, a steering mechanism, and seating that doesn't require straddling — https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_501.008
- No motorcycle license required to drive an autocycle in Texas — Tex. Transp. Code §521.085(b): https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_521.085
- Standard safety equipment: ABS, traction control, electronic stability control, electronic power steering — Wikipedia / vincheck 2024 Slingshot specs: https://vincheck.info/report/polaris/slingshot/2024
- Curb weight approximately 1,651 lb — `seo/research/audience-market-research.md` §6c
- No airbags, no roll cage — a rear roll hoop exists behind the seats but is not a cage — `seo/research/audience-market-research.md` §2c / Wikipedia
- Reverse trike vs. delta (1 front/2 rear) layouts are the two common 3-wheeler configurations — general reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-wheeler
