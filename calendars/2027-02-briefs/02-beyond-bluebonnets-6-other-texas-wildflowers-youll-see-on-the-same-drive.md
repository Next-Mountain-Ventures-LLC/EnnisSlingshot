## Facts & sources to cite
- Bluebonnets are *Lupinus texensis*, the Texas state flower — Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center, https://www.wildflower.org (cited in serp-intelligence.md §1 "bluebonnet season texas" Knowledge Graph).
- Ennis trails run April 1–30 across 40+ miles of mapped routes — bluebonnettrail.org; elliscountytx.gov (business-context.md).
- Bluebonnet peak bloom is typically the third week of April, varies by weather/terrain — visitennistexas.com; bluebonnettrail.org/trailmap (audience-market-research.md §4a) — use this as the general timing anchor for the companion species too, with a caveat that non-bluebonnet species can bloom slightly earlier/later.
- 2026 season was predicted "moderate" after a dry fall and warm winter; bluebonnets germinate in fall and depend on fall/winter rain — FOX 7 Austin; CultureMap (audience-market-research.md §4a) — relevant context for why companion wildflowers vary year to year too.
- Named photo/viewing locations on the trails: Meadow View Nature Area, Bluebonnet Park, Lakeview Drive/Bardwell Lake area, Sugar Ridge Road, Kachina Prairie — audience-market-research.md §4a.
- **VERIFY before publishing:** individual species bloom windows, identifying features, and Texas-range specifics for Indian paintbrush (*Castilleja indivisa*), pink evening primrose (*Oenothera speciosa*), coreopsis, Indian blanket/firewheel (*Gaillardia pulchella*), prairie verbena, and winecup — pull each from its wildflower.org species page (https://www.wildflower.org/plants) rather than from memory.
- Is it illegal to pick bluebonnets/wildflowers in Texas — no state law bans it, but posted private property and highway right-of-way rules apply — see the dedicated earlier post (bb-035, Jan 21) rather than restating the legal analysis here; link to it.
