## Facts & sources to cite
- Tex. Transp. Code §661.0015(b): "an autocycle is considered to be a motorcycle" for helmet-law purposes, except autocycles with a partially or fully enclosed cab (a stock Slingshot has none) — https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_661.0015
- Tex. Transp. Code §661.003: riders under 21 must wear a helmet; riders 21+ are exempt only if they completed a motorcycle operator training/safety course **or** carry a health insurance plan covering collision injuries; fine for noncompliance is $10–$50; police "may not stop or detain" solely to check — https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_661.003
- Tex. Transp. Code §501.008(a): defines "autocycle" as a 3-wheel motor vehicle with seat belts, a steering wheel, and seating that doesn't require straddling — the classification that triggers the motorcycle helmet chapter — https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_501.008
- Tex. Transp. Code §521.085(b): confirms a standard Class C license (not a motorcycle license) covers autocycle operation — cited briefly for context, full discussion in the Sep 15 laws pillar — https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_521.085
- Texas DPS, "New Law Establishes Definition, Rules for Autocycles" — plain-language state confirmation of the autocycle classification — https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/1439
- XINSURANCE, "Tips for Slingshot Rental Businesses" — industry-standard operator practice of requiring helmets/eye protection regardless of the legal exemption, and 21+ driver norms — https://www.xinsurance.com/blog/tips-for-slingshot-rental-businesses/
